I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at UC Merced, where I study how historical trauma and victimhood narratives shape political behavior. My work spans from voting behavior within nations to international land disputes.

My dissertation asks how a single polity remembers more than one historical trauma at once. Drawing on original archival data from South Korea on Japanese colonial forced labor and Korean War civilian killings, it combines spatial analysis of election returns, a nationally representative survey experiment, and an agent-based model of memory transmission. Related projects examine how land claims shape outsider support in conflict and how victimhood narratives drive polarization.

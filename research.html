Dissertation

Job Market Paper

"Not One Story: Multiple Historical Trauma and the Politics of Remembering"

My job market paper asks how a single polity remembers more than one historical trauma at once. Using an original township-level dataset built from Japanese colonial forced-labor conscription rosters (National Archives of Korea) and Korean War civilian killing records (Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Korea), I harmonize administrative boundaries from 1945 onward and estimate how local exposure to each trauma shapes presidential voting across six elections (2002–2025). The results show that the perpetrator identity of a trauma, rather than its magnitude, determines whether and how it registers politically, and that colonial and wartime traumas leave separable rather than compounding traces.

Survey Experiment

Priming colonial, wartime, and layered memory among South Korean adults

To recover the individual-level mechanisms behind the observational findings, I designed a nationally representative survey experiment in South Korea (n = 2,000) that primes colonial memory, wartime memory, or both. The instrument, vignettes, and pre-analysis plan are complete, and the survey is scheduled for fielding in fall 2026.

Memory in Motion

"An Agent-Based Model of Trauma, Transmission, and Cleavage Formation"

This chapter formalizes how traumatic memory is transmitted across generations and space, and under what conditions it hardens into a durable political cleavage. The model is calibrated to the empirical estimates from the first two chapters.

Christopher Ojeda, Youngbin Kim, & Victoria Davis. "Political Ideology and Perceptions of Mental Illness." Perspectives on Politics (under review).

Using the General Social Survey mental health vignette module, this paper examines how political ideology shapes what people believe causes mental illness, how they label it, and what treatment they consider appropriate.

Jingyeong Song, Youngbin Kim, & Awais Khan. "Do Online Petitions Frame Public Perceptions? An Association Rule Mining Analysis on Yemeni Refugees in South Korea." Information Polity (under review).

Analyzing 1,310 South Korean presidential petitions filed after the 2018 arrival of Yemeni asylum seekers on Jeju, this paper uses association rule mining to show how petitions constructed a frame linking refugees to security and economic threats rather than humanitarian concerns.

Working Papers

Empathetic Grounds

"How Land Claim Shapes Outsider Support and Justifies Actions in Conflict" (solo-authored)

This project investigates how a claim to land shapes the support of outside observers for parties in conflict. Study 1, a pre-registered vignette experiment (n = 1,986), finds that a land claim shifts support, perceived legitimacy, and perceived victimhood strongly toward the claimant. Study 2, pre-registered and awaiting fielding, tests whether these effects persist after fact-checking and traces their emotional and behavioral echoes.

Draft (Forthcoming)

Youngbin Kim & Raymond Kim. "Korean Free Economic Zones (KFEZs) and Regional Unemployment: Application of Generalized Synthetic Control."

This paper estimates the causal effect of Korean Free Economic Zones on regional unemployment, applying a generalized synthetic control approach to monthly region-level data across South Korea.

Youngbin Kim, Christopher Ojeda, & Victoria Davis. "Cross-National Evidence on Ideology and Physical Health Perceptions."

Extending the mental health project cross-nationally, this paper uses ISSP data from 20 countries (2011 and 2021) to test whether beliefs about the causes of physical illness are politically polarized, and finds that conservatives emphasize individual causes while liberals emphasize structural ones across most countries.

Sung Min Yun, Youngbin Kim, & Shimaa Hanafy. "Military Counterbalancing and State Repression: An Empirical Investigation."

Using a cross-national panel of 108 countries (1961–2011), this paper examines whether parallel armed forces created to counterbalance the regular military increase state repression.
